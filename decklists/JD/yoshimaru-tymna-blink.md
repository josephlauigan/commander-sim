# Yoshimaru, Ever Faithful & Tymna the Weaver — Blink and Enter-the-Battlefield Value (W/B)

Put creatures with enter effects on the battlefield, then use them again. Solitude, Skyclave Apparition, Palace Jailer, Ranger-Captain of Eos, Recruiter of the Guard and Sun Titan each do their job every time they come back, and ten blink cards bring them back. A life layer (Elas il-Kor, Haliya, Syr Vondam, Vito, Kambal, Sheoldred) turns every enter and every blink into drain, and Tymna with Delney draws twice as many cards. Four short lines can end the game: Vito with Exquisite Blood, Heliod with Walking Ballista, Archangel of Thune with Walking Ballista and a lifelink source, and Restoration Angel with Felidar Guardian and a payoff. Yoshimaru's counters are a bonus, not the engine: about a 5/5 by turn 6 (an estimate). The deck is weak to effects that stop enter triggers, to fast combo (its answers are Silence, Grand Abolisher, Ranger-Captain, instant removal and, against creature and nonbasic-land combos, Thalia, and it has no counterspell), to graveyard and stax effects, to a sweeper when it taps out, and to life loss. **This list has not been simulated; every number below is a count from the list or a labelled estimate.**

## Strategy

### The commanders

**Yoshimaru, Ever Faithful** ({W}, 1/1). *Whenever another legendary permanent you control enters, put a +1/+1 counter on Yoshimaru. Partner.*

**Tymna the Weaver** ({1}{W}{B}, 2/2). *Lifelink. At the beginning of each of your postcombat main phases, you may pay X life, where X is the number of opponents that were dealt combat damage this turn. If you do, draw X cards. Partner.*

Both have Partner and share the command zone. Each costs {2} more for every time it has been cast from there before.

- **Yoshimaru is a one-mana body that grows.** He counts every legendary permanent we play: 26 in the 98 (4 lands, 15 creatures, 2 artifacts, 4 planeswalkers and 1 enchantment, listed under Decklist by type), and every blink of one of them is another enter. He has no evasion and no protection of his own, so he is a bonus, not the plan (see Yoshimaru's counters below).
- **Tymna is the card engine, but only if she connects.** X is the number of opponents hit by combat damage from any of our creatures that turn, so a flier, a token or a creature that cannot be blocked draws her cards. With Delney she draws twice (see Doubling triggers). Her lifelink is a life gain on every hit, which feeds Vito, Heliod and Archangel of Thune.

**The order rule.** Yoshimaru must be on the battlefield first. His trigger is for *another* legendary permanent that enters while he is there, so Tymna entering after him is his first counter. If Tymna is out first, Yoshimaru's own entry gets nothing, and neither does anything that came down before him. With four mana and neither commander out, cast Yoshimaru, then Tymna.

**When to cast each.**

- **Yoshimaru on turn 1** with a white land that enters untapped. Twenty lands in the list qualify: 10 Plains, Scrubland, Caves of Koilos, Command Tower, Eiganjo, Vault of Champions (untapped with two or more opponents), Plaza of Heroes (any colour for a legendary spell), and Godless Shrine (2 life), Witch Enchanter // Witch-Blessed Meadow (3 life), Marsh Flats and Arid Mesa (fetch an untapped Plains) at a small life cost. Minas Tirith (tapped without a legendary creature) and Isolated Chapel (tapped unless we control a Plains or a Swamp) enter tapped as a first land, and Urborg, Takenuma and the Swamps make no white. Chance of at least one of the 20 in the opening hand: 80.9% on the play (7 cards), 85.1% on the draw (8 cards).
- **Only Chrome Mox lets Yoshimaru come down before the land drop.** Chrome Mox ({0}): when it enters we may exile a nonland, nonartifact card from our hand, and it taps for one mana of a colour of that card, so with a white card imprinted it pays {W}: Mox, Yoshimaru, then the land. That order also lets a legendary land (Eiganjo, Takenuma, Urborg, Minas Tirith) come down after Yoshimaru as his first counter, and Minas Tirith then enters untapped. Sol Ring and Mana Vault make colourless mana and cannot pay {W}, and Mox Amber makes mana only of a colour among our legendary creatures and planeswalkers, so it needs a legend already on the battlefield. Without Chrome Mox the turn-1 order is land, then Yoshimaru, and his first counter comes on turn 2 from a legendary land, a legendary rock, or Tymna.
- **Chrome Mox costs a card.** 60 of the 98 cards can be imprinted (every nonland, nonartifact card; Witch Enchanter counts, since in hand it is a creature card; 50 are white and 18 black, and none is colourless), so there is almost always a card to give, but the card is gone for good. Imprint a white or black card we will not cast soon (a six-drop, a second sweeper or a spare protection spell) and pick the card whose colour the hand is missing; with no such card in hand the Mox makes no mana. With an untapped white land on turn 1 it is two mana: Yoshimaru ({W}) and a one-drop ({W}) such as Mother of Runes or Giver of Runes. It is an artifact, so Enlightened Tutor can find it.
- **Mox Amber right after Yoshimaru** is a free legendary permanent entering (a counter) and then taps for {W}, because Yoshimaru is a white legendary creature. That {W} can cast Mother of Runes, Giver of Runes, Enlightened Tutor or Ephemerate, or pay for Sol Ring on turn 1.
- **Tymna on turn 3**, normally. With Mox Amber and a black source she can come down on turn 2: {W} from Mox Amber (Yoshimaru), {B} from a land, {1} from the other. Chrome Mox does the same job with a white or black card imprinted: with Yoshimaru out on turn 1, two lands and the Mox on turn 2 pay {1}{W}{B} when the lands and the imprint cover both colours. Plaza of Heroes can also supply one coloured mana for her, since she is a legendary spell.
- **Tymna can't attack the turn she comes down, but she can still draw that turn.** Her trigger needs only that she is on the battlefield at the start of the postcombat main phase, and X counts the opponents dealt combat damage by any creature of ours. Cast her before combat and she draws for Yoshimaru's, Adeline's or a flier's hits. Cast Delney before her, so that the doubling is already there.

**Which to protect.** Yoshimaru costs {W} to replace and loses his counters whenever he leaves; Tymna costs {1}{W}{B} plus {2} per recast and carries the card engine. Spend Mother of Runes, Giver of Runes, Ephemerate and Restoration Angel on Tymna first, unless Yoshimaru is already large.

3. **Tymna with Delney, and the cards that come with the board.** Delney, Streetwise Lookout makes Tymna's trigger fire twice (see Doubling triggers). Welcoming Vampire, Mentor of the Meek, Inspiring Overseer, Haliya, Palace Jailer's monarchy and The One Ring add cards around her. Welcoming Vampire draws once each turn when creatures with power 2 or less enter; Mentor of the Meek draws (for {1}) for each such creature; both reward a blink of a small creature.

**Ten blink pieces:** Ephemerate, Restoration Angel, Flickerwisp, Felidar Guardian, Charming Prince, Eerie Interlude, Teleportation Circle, Conjurer's Closet, Ennis and The Eternal Wanderer. **Three doublers:** Delney, Gandalf the White and Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines.

**Conjurer's Closet** ({5}, a colourless artifact, not legendary): *At the beginning of your end step, you may exile target creature you control, then return that card to the battlefield under your control.* It blinks only creatures (not artifacts or other permanents), only at the beginning of our own end step, one target, and it is a "may". It costs 5 and does nothing until a creature is on the battlefield, so it is a late-game engine and a reason the deck wants good creatures (Solitude, Skyclave Apparition, Palace Jailer, Sun Titan, Ranger-Captain of Eos, Recruiter of the Guard) to repeat. Cast in one of our main phases it already blinks at that turn's end step; Gandalf's flash for artifact spells lets us cast it during combat, or at an opponent's end step so that it is ready for our next turn. It is an artifact entering, not a legend, so it gives Yoshimaru no counter. It does set off cards that look for an artifact or any permanent entering (Haliya gains 1 life, and Gandalf and Elesh Norn each add a trigger, see Doubling triggers). Its own end-step trigger is not caused by anything entering and is not a creature's trigger, so no doubler adds a trigger to it; the blinked creature's enter effect is doubled as usual. It stacks with Teleportation Circle (see The end-step engine below).

**The One Ring** gives protection from everything for a turn when cast, then a card engine that costs life every upkeep (see Mana, life costs). **Lotho, Corrupt Shirriff** ({W}{B}, a legendary 2/1): *Whenever a player casts their second spell each turn, you lose 1 life and create a Treasure token.* He is a legendary creature, so he gives Yoshimaru a counter when he enters after him, and every blink of Lotho is a legendary permanent entering again. He has no enter effect of his own, so he does nothing for blink value: he is a two-drop that grows Yoshimaru and makes mana. The trigger is for any player's second spell each turn, opponents included, so at a four-player table it fires often, and the life loss is always ours and cannot be declined. With Delney out it fires twice (Lotho has power 2 and the trigger is a triggered ability): 2 life and 2 Treasures for each second spell. A Treasure is one mana of any colour (ramp and fixing, including for a second spell of our own) and an artifact token entering under our control, so Haliya's "Whenever Haliya or another creature or artifact you control enters, you gain 1 life" fires for it, and Gandalf (an artifact entering) and Elesh Norn (a permanent entering) each add one more Haliya trigger: 1 life per Treasure, 2 with one of them, 3 with both. That gain is a life gain like any other for Vito, Heliod and Archangel of Thune. Elas il-Kor triggers only on creatures, so a Treasure does nothing for her, and a Treasure is not legendary, so it gives Yoshimaru nothing. Gandalf and Elesh Norn do not double Lotho himself, since his trigger is caused by a spell being cast and not by anything entering. **The cost is real:** this deck already pays life (Tymna, fetch and shock lands, the tutors, The One Ring), and Lotho adds a loss that the whole table sets off, so keep an eye on life with him out. With Haliya out each Treasure also gains 1 life; without her the loss comes out of the same life total as everything else.

**Thalia, Heretic Cathar** ({2}{W}, a legendary 3/2 with first strike): *Creatures and nonbasic lands your opponents control enter tapped.* She is the deck's slowdown for the table. Haste creatures and creature combo pieces cannot attack or use a tap ability the turn they arrive, an opposing creature token enters tapped, and a shock land, a fetched dual or a utility land enters tapped, which costs the table a turn of mana. Only opponents' permanents are affected: our own creatures and lands enter untapped, so Yoshimaru, Tymna and every enter effect work as before. She does nothing against spells, artifacts (rocks and Treasures enter untapped), planeswalkers or a combo that needs no creature and no nonbasic land. She is a legendary creature, so she gives Yoshimaru a counter when she enters after him, and a blink of her is another legend entering; she has no enter effect of her own, so a blink adds the counter and the life layer's triggers (Elas il-Kor and Haliya gain 1 life each, and Syr Vondam gets a counter and 1 life when she is exiled) and nothing of her own. Her power is 3, so Delney does not help her and she does not trigger Welcoming Vampire or Mentor of the Meek; Recruiter of the Guard can find her (toughness 2). A 3/2 first striker attacks or blocks well: it kills most two-drops without dying.

**The Eternal Wanderer** is both a blink piece and a sweeper: its -4 has us choose one creature for each player, and every player sacrifices the rest, so we keep our best creature and leave each opponent their weakest. Its starting loyalty is not in the data, so nothing here depends on it.

#### Yoshimaru's counters

Be honest about this part: he is a bonus. All numbers are estimates and the deck has not been simulated.

- **Inputs.** The library is 98 cards (the commanders are in the command zone) with 26 legendary permanents. By turn 6 on the play we have seen 12 cards (7 + 5 draws; 13 on the draw). Tymna's draws and the tutors are ignored.
- **Legends seen.** The chance of seeing at least 2, 3, 4 and 5 of the 26 in 12 cards is 88.4%, 67.0%, 39.7% and 17.7% (hypergeometric, exact), a mean of 3.18.
- **Legends played.** Not every legend seen is cast by turn 6 (cost, colours, a land drop used on something else). Assuming 75% are played gives 2.39 counters on average, and the chance of at least 2, 3 and 4 played becomes 73.5%, 44.1% and 19.5%. Playing 50% gives 1.59 and 100% gives 3.18.
- **Tymna** gives one counter only when Yoshimaru is already out. Assuming that is true in 90% of games: +0.9.
- **Blinks.** The 10 blink pieces give a mean of 1.22 in 12 cards. Assuming half are cast with a legend on the battlefield by turn 6, and each makes 1.5 legendary re-entries on average (Ephemerate rebounds, Eerie Interlude takes several, Teleportation Circle repeats): +0.92.
- **Total:** about 2.39 + 0.9 + 0.92 = 4.2 counters, a range of about 3.1 to 5.6 counters (low: half of the legends seen are played and blinks add 0.6; high: all are played and blinks add 1.5). Yoshimaru is a 1/1 base, so about a 5/5 by turn 6.
- **Doublers.** The chance of at least one of Delney, Gandalf and Elesh Norn in 12 cards is 32.7%; each adds one counter per legend entering after it lands (Delney only while Yoshimaru's power is 2 or less).
- **Reading.** A big ground body with no evasion that the table will remove if it can. Commander damage (21) from a 5/5 needs five hits and is not a plan. The deck does not win by his size.

#### Doubling triggers: Gandalf, Elesh Norn and Delney

Card texts. **Gandalf the White** ({3}{W}{W}, 4/5): *Flash. You may cast legendary spells and artifact spells as though they had flash. If a legendary permanent or an artifact entering or leaving the battlefield causes a triggered ability of a permanent you control to trigger, that ability triggers an additional time.* **Elesh Norn, Mother of Machines** ({4}{W}, 4/7): *Vigilance. If a permanent entering causes a triggered ability of a permanent you control to trigger, that ability triggers an additional time. Permanents entering don't cause abilities of permanents your opponents control to trigger.* **Delney, Streetwise Lookout** ({2}{W}, 2/2): *Creatures you control with power 2 or less can't be blocked by creatures with power 3 or greater. If a triggered ability of a creature you control with power 2 or less triggers, that ability triggers an additional time.*

Each doubler adds one extra trigger; they do not multiply each other (the usual ruling for effects of this kind, a rules point and not card text: two such effects give three triggers).

**Yoshimaru's counters** (his trigger is caused by a legendary permanent entering):

| Doublers on the battlefield | Counters per legendary permanent that enters |
|---|---|
| None | 1 |
| Gandalf only | 2 (a legendary permanent is always legendary) |
| Elesh Norn only | 2 (a permanent entering) |
| Delney only | 2 while Yoshimaru's power is 2 or less, otherwise 1 |
| Gandalf and Elesh Norn | 3 |
| All three, Yoshimaru with 0 or 1 counter | 4 |
| All three, Yoshimaru with 2 or more counters | 3 |

Yoshimaru is a 1/1, so 0 counters is power 1 and 1 counter is power 2. At 2 counters his power is 3 and Delney stops doubling him; Delney's evasion for him stops too. A blinked legend is a legend entering again, so the same table applies each time.

**Tymna with Delney.** Tymna is a 2/2, so her trigger fires twice at each postcombat main phase. Each copy asks for its own payment, so with X opponents hit we pay X life and draw X cards, twice: 2X cards for 2X life. It works only while Tymna's power is 2 or less when the trigger happens. What turns it off:

- A counter on Tymna: Archangel of Thune, whose trigger puts a counter on every creature each time we gain life (Tymna's own lifelink hit gains life, so with Thune out she grows past power 2 before her postcombat trigger); Heliod, if we aim its counter at her; The Wandering Emperor's +1.
- Elspeth's +3/+3 on her, which lasts until end of turn and so covers the postcombat main phase.
- Removal on Delney, or Delney blinked out or phased out when the trigger would happen.

Gandalf and Elesh Norn do not double Tymna or Lotho: their triggers are caused by a phase beginning (Tymna) and by a spell being cast (Lotho), not by anything entering. Sorin's lifelink changes nothing for her (she already has it and her power is unchanged).

**Delney and Archangel of Thune mostly cancel each other.** Each life gain puts a counter on every creature, so a power-2 creature goes past the limit on the first gain and a power-1 creature on the second. With Thune out, one Elas, Haliya or Overseer gain before combat turns the doubling off for the whole team that turn. Treat Thune as a finisher (Line C) or a Line D payoff, not as something to cast on a turn where Tymna needs Delney. With Heliod aim the counter at Heliod himself (he is an enchantment we control) or at a large creature, not at Tymna or the small creatures.

**Elesh Norn covers the rest:** every enter trigger of ours fires an extra time, including Solitude, Ranger-Captain, Restoration Angel, Flickerwisp and Sun Titan. With Elesh Norn and Delney both out, a power-2 creature with an enter trigger fires three times (Recruiter of the Guard: three searches). With Gandalf too, a legendary creature of power 2 or less fires four times (Loran: four destroys).

**Elas with Delney, Elesh Norn and Vito.** Elas has power 2 and Vito power 1. Each other creature entering gives Elas 1 + 1 (Elesh Norn) + 1 (Delney) = 3 gains of 1 life. Each gain triggers Vito twice (1 + 1 for Delney; Elesh Norn does not apply, since a life gain is not an entering), so 3 gains lose an opponent 6 life per creature that enters. If the creature is legendary or an artifact, Gandalf adds a fourth Elas trigger: 8 life. Four cards, so this is something to build toward and not a requirement.

**Gandalf also doubles:** Elas for a legendary or artifact creature entering or dying, Syr Vondam for a legendary or artifact creature dying or being exiled, and Haliya for a legendary creature or artifact entering (a Lotho Treasure is an artifact entering). Gandalf's flash for legendary and artifact spells lets Heliod, Walking Ballista, Mox Amber, Tymna, Conjurer's Closet and the rocks come down at an opponent's end step. **Conjurer's Closet** entering is an artifact entering: Haliya's trigger fires for it (1 life), and Gandalf (an artifact entering) and Elesh Norn (a permanent entering) each add one trigger to it. It is not a legendary permanent, so it does not trigger Yoshimaru, and Delney does nothing for it (it is not a creature).

### How the deck wins

Four lines can end the game, and the grind sets them up. **The numbers are mana counts from the list, not win rates.** A trigger that is "may" can be declined; Vito's and Exquisite Blood's triggers are not.

#### Line A: Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose + Exquisite Blood

Two cards, 8 mana ({2}{B} and {4}{B}), over one turn or two. Vito: *Whenever you gain life, target opponent loses that much life.* Exquisite Blood: *Whenever an opponent loses life, you gain that much life.*

1. Cast **Vito**. It works alone: every life gain is a drain.
2. Cast **Exquisite Blood** when a start is ready, or when no opponent can answer it before our next turn.
3. **Start the loop.** The cast of the second piece does not start it. We need one life gain, or one point of life lost by an opponent, after both are on the battlefield: Elas's gain for a creature entering, Haliya, Inspiring Overseer, Charming Prince's 3 life, Kaya's Wrath, The Wandering Emperor's -2, a Sheoldred draw, Tymna's lifelink hit, Kambal's trigger, an Orcish Bowmasters or Walking Ballista ping, Sorin's +2, the Meathook Massacre, or an opponent paying life for a fetch land or a shock land. A cheap start with Elas out is a one-drop creature such as Mother of Runes ({W}): Elas gains 1 life when it enters.
4. **The loop.** An opponent loses N, Blood gains us N, Vito makes an opponent lose N, Blood gains us N again. Both triggers are mandatory, and each Vito trigger targets one opponent; when that opponent dies, the next trigger targets another. The loop ends only when every opponent is dead.

**What stops it:** a counterspell on the second piece; removal on Vito or on Blood (a trigger already on the stack still resolves, so one more cycle can happen before the loop stops); an effect that stops life changes (including our own Teferi's Protection); an opponent who can't lose life or can't be targeted.

**Finding it:** Enlightened Tutor finds Exquisite Blood; Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor and Imperial Seal find either piece. Nothing else finds Vito: he is a creature that is neither an artifact nor an enchantment, and his toughness of 3 is out of Recruiter of the Guard's range.

#### Line B: Heliod, Sun-Crowned + Walking Ballista

Two cards, 9 mana: Heliod 3, Walking Ballista with X = 2 for 4, and {1}{W} for lifelink. Heliod: *Whenever you gain life, put a +1/+1 counter on target creature or enchantment you control. {1}{W}: Another target creature gains lifelink until end of turn.* Walking Ballista: *This creature enters with X +1/+1 counters on it. {4}: Put a +1/+1 counter on this creature. Remove a +1/+1 counter from this creature: It deals 1 damage to any target.*

1. Heliod and Ballista (X = 2) on the battlefield.
2. {1}{W}: Ballista gains lifelink until end of turn.
3. Remove a counter (Ballista is a 1/1): 1 damage to an opponent. Lifelink: we gain 1. Heliod's trigger: a +1/+1 counter on Ballista (back to 2 counters).
4. Repeat. Each cycle costs no mana and takes 1 life from an opponent (with Vito out each gain is also a drain). The table's life totals are the only limit.

**Why X = 2:** at X = 1, removing the counter makes Ballista a 0/0 that dies, so Heliod's counter has no Ballista to land on and the loop ends after one cycle.

**What stops it:** removal on Ballista before it has lifelink (answer by pinging with every counter in response); removal on Ballista in response to Heliod's trigger (the counter has no target); Teferi's Protection or a life-gain prevention effect; a counterspell. Heliod himself is indestructible, so only exile or bounce stops him.

**Finding it:** Enlightened Tutor finds either (both are artifact or enchantment cards); Ranger-Captain of Eos finds Ballista (mana value 0 in the library); Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor and Imperial Seal find either.

#### Line C: Archangel of Thune + Walking Ballista + a lifelink source

Three pieces. Archangel of Thune: *Whenever you gain life, put a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control.* Lifelink on Ballista can come from Sorin ({2}{W}{B}: during our turn our creatures have lifelink), Heliod ({1}{W}) or Vito's {3}{B}{B}.

1. Thune and Ballista (X = 2) on the battlefield, and Sorin out (or Heliod's lifelink, or Vito's activation) so that Ballista has lifelink on our turn.
2. Remove a counter: 1 damage with lifelink, we gain 1, Thune puts a counter on each of our creatures (Ballista is back to 2).
3. Repeat: the same loop as Line B with Thune in Heliod's place.

Costs 5 + 4 + 4 = 13 mana with Sorin, over two turns. It is the second way to run the Ballista loop when Heliod is not there, and it grows the whole team (watch Tymna: her power goes past 2, which turns Delney's doubling off).

#### Line D: Restoration Angel + Felidar Guardian

Two cards that blink each other. Restoration Angel: *When this creature enters, you may exile target non-Angel creature you control, then return that card to the battlefield under your control.* Felidar Guardian: *When this creature enters, you may exile another target permanent you control, then return that card to the battlefield under its owner's control.*

1. Restoration Angel on the battlefield, then cast Felidar Guardian (or the reverse).
2. Felidar's trigger exiles Restoration Angel and returns it. Restoration Angel's trigger exiles Felidar (a Cat Beast, not an Angel) and returns it. Felidar's trigger exiles Restoration Angel again. Each trip makes both creatures enter, and both triggers are "may", so we choose when to stop.
3. **The payoff is separate.** Without one the loop does nothing. With a payoff on the battlefield:
   - **Elas il-Kor, Haliya or Syr Vondam alone** gives infinite life (Vondam also grows without limit, and has menace).
   - **Add Vito** and the life becomes a kill.
   - **Add Archangel of Thune** instead and every creature gets infinite counters, and Restoration Angel and Thune fly.
4. **Flash timing.** Restoration Angel has flash. With Felidar already on the battlefield, cast Restoration Angel at an opponent's end step and run the loop there, with the payoff already out.
5. **Ending the loop.** Aim the last Felidar trigger at Recruiter of the Guard (a search), Solitude or Skyclave Apparition (removal), or decline the "may".

Cost: 8 mana for the two cards, then the payoff already on the battlefield. The worst case is 13 mana and four cards (Restoration Angel 4, Felidar 4, Elas 2, Vito 3); it is a late finisher or a finish when two of the pieces are already on the table. **What stops it:** removal on either creature in response to a trigger (the trigger has no target), an effect that stops enter triggers, or removal on the payoff. Teferi's Protection, again, stops the life changes.

#### The grind

Expect most games to be won here. Keep the engine running: one enter effect per turn, a blink for the best one, Tymna connecting with Delney out, a drain on every creature that enters, and the monarchy from Palace Jailer. Solitude, Skyclave Apparition and Palace Jailer clear blockers; Welcoming Vampire, Inspiring Overseer, Restoration Angel, Flickerwisp and Archangel of Thune fly; Delney makes every creature with power 2 or less (Tymna, Elas, Vito, Adeline's Human tokens, Elspeth's Soldiers) unblockable by creatures with power 3 or more. Three opponents at 40 is a lot of life: the grind's job is to land Vito, Elas and Kambal, keep both commanders alive, and set up a line. Yoshimaru's size is a side effect.

**Enter effects and blink work like this:**

| Piece | Target | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Ephemerate ({W}, instant) | A creature we control | Rebound: cast from hand, it comes back at the next upkeep for free: two blinks for one card. Also a response to removal |
| Restoration Angel ({3}{W}, flash) | A non-Angel creature we control | The enter effect itself, at instant speed |
| Sunpearl Kirin ({1}{W}, flash) | Up to one other nonland permanent we control | Not a blink: the permanent goes to hand and must be recast, so it costs mana. Its own enter effect is doubled by Delney or Elesh Norn |
| Charming Prince | Another creature we own | Returns at the beginning of the next end step |
| Ennis | Another creature we control | Returns at the beginning of the next end step |
| Flickerwisp | Any other permanent | Mandatory. Ours is a delayed enter effect. An opposing permanent is gone until the end step, and an opposing token is gone for good (a rules point). Elesh Norn stops its owner's enter trigger when it returns |
| Felidar Guardian | Any other permanent we control | Works on planeswalkers (a blinked planeswalker is a new object with its starting loyalty and can use an ability again, a rules point), rocks |
| Eerie Interlude ({2}{W}, instant) | Any number of our creatures | They return at the beginning of the next end step: cast it before the end step you want them back at. It is a mass enter effect and the sweeper answer |

**The end-step engine.** Two permanents blink at the beginning of our own end step: Teleportation Circle (up to one target artifact or creature we control) and Conjurer's Closet (one target creature we control, a "may"). They trigger at the same time, each is a separate trigger, and we choose the order they go on the stack (a rules point, not card text). They stack by blinking two different permanents in one end step. Aim each trigger at a different target: a creature that an earlier trigger has already returned is a new object, and a later trigger aimed at the old one does nothing (a rules point, not card text). The Closet takes only creatures; the Circle also reaches artifacts.

**What to blink at the end step:** Recruiter of the Guard or Ranger-Captain of Eos (a search every turn), Sun Titan (a permanent card with mana value 3 or less from the graveyard), Palace Jailer (a new exile and the monarchy back), Solitude (an exile), Inspiring Overseer (a card), and a creature that makes life or counters with the life layer out. The cases below are the exceptions. A creature whose own effect returns things "at the beginning of the next end step" behaves differently when it is blinked at our end step: that end step's beginning has already passed, so the return waits for the next turn's end step (a rules point, not card text). Flickerwisp blinked this way keeps the opposing permanent it exiled gone through that opponent's whole turn, which is a strong use; Ennis or Charming Prince blinking our own creature keeps it away during their turn, safe from a sorcery-speed sweeper but not available as a blocker.

**What not to blink:**

- **Walking Ballista.** It re-enters with X = 0 and dies (a rules point: with no cast, X is 0).
- **Skyclave Apparition**, unless the exiled card was cheap. Blinking it makes the exiled card's owner create an X/X Illusion token (X is that card's mana value) and gives us a new exile. The old card stays exiled.
- **Yoshimaru himself.** He re-enters with no counters. Blink him only to answer removal, and protect him with Mother of Runes, Giver of Runes or Plaza of Heroes first.
- **The One Ring is a neutral case, not a mistake.** Blinking it gives no protection (the protection needs a cast), and it comes back with no burden counters (a rules point): that stops the life loss and also resets the card count, so it is a way to stop the life loss when it gets too high.
- **Palace Jailer** is good to blink (it exiles again and takes the monarchy back), but the first exile lasts only until an opponent becomes the monarch, not while Jailer stays.
- **Solitude** is the best target for a blink: with the evoke cast (exile a white card, free), a blink in response to the sacrifice trigger leaves it on the battlefield as a new object that was not evoked, with a second exile. Ephemerate, Restoration Angel and Eerie Interlude all work (rules points, not card text).

**Haliya's warp.** For {W}, Haliya enters (a legend for Yoshimaru, and 1 life), is exiled at the beginning of the next end step (a counter and 1 life for Syr Vondam), and can be cast from exile on a later turn (another legend entering). It is a cheap extra enter, and a cheap blink-like play on turn 1 or 2.

### Game plan

**Turn 1.** A white land that enters untapped, then Yoshimaru; Mox Amber right after him if drawn (counter and a free {W}). With Chrome Mox in hand, play it first (imprinting a white card) and spend its {W} and the land's on Yoshimaru and a one-drop. **Turn 2.** A two-drop that does something when it enters (Charming Prince, Ennis; Orcish Bowmasters or Sunpearl Kirin at an opponent's end step), or Elas, Syr Vondam, Lotho ({W}{B}, a legendary two-drop that is a counter for Yoshimaru if he is already out), a rock, or Demonic Tutor. **Turn 3.** Tymna, or Delney first if both are in hand and a blinker is ready. **Turns 4 to 6.** An enter-effect creature a turn, with a blink to repeat the best one. Hold flash plays (Restoration Angel, Solitude, Bowmasters, The Wandering Emperor, Gandalf) for an opponent's end step. **Late.** The 8 to 13 mana lines, and Sun Titan, Eternal Wanderer and Conjurer's Closet as the late plays.

**Go now or hold?**

- **Go now** when a line is complete (Line A with a start in hand, Line B with 9 mana, Line D at an opponent's end step), or when the table has tapped out.
- **Hold** the second piece of Line A until a start is ready; every turn Vito or Blood sits alone is a turn for an answer to find it.
- **Hold** a blink spell for a removal spell or a sweeper when a creature that matters is on the table: the spell is worth more as an answer than as a second enter.
- **Hold** Eerie Interlude and Teferi's Protection when the table plays sweepers.

A commander in the command zone can be recast for its tax. Yoshimaru is cheap to recast but loses his counters.

**Sweepers, and what each answer does not stop.** A destroy sweeper (Kaya's Wrath type) is stopped by Flawless Maneuver, Selfless Spirit, Plaza on a legend, Eerie Interlude and Teferi's Protection. A -X/-X sweeper (Toxic Deluge type) is stopped only by Eerie Interlude and Teferi's Protection (an instant blink does not help: the creature returns before the sweeper resolves, so it still gets -X/-X). Exile and bounce effects are stopped by the same two. After a wipe the deck rebuilds with its noncreature permanents: the four planeswalkers, Teleportation Circle, The One Ring, Heliod (not a creature unless our devotion to white is five or more), Exquisite Blood, The Meathook Massacre, Conjurer's Closet (it needs a creature to blink), plus Sun Titan from hand, Takenuma's channel (returns a creature or planeswalker from the graveyard) and Sorin's -X (the rest of its text is cut off in the data and is not relied on). The draw engines are creatures, so a wipe also costs the card flow.

**Against combo.** The deck has no counterspell. Its stops are:

- **Silence** ({W}, instant): *Your opponents can't cast spells this turn.* Cast it in an opponent's upkeep and they cast nothing that turn. It does not stop abilities (Walking Ballista, a planeswalker, a land) or spells already on the stack.
- **Grand Abolisher** ({W}{W}, 2/2): *During your turn, your opponents can't cast spells or activate abilities of artifacts, creatures, or enchantments.* It covers a whole combo turn of ours and a Restoration Angel and Felidar loop run on our own turn, but it is a creature spell that can be answered before it matters, and it does nothing on their turn.
- **Ranger-Captain of Eos** ({1}{W}{W}, 3/3): *Sacrifice this creature: Your opponents can't cast noncreature spells this turn.* It works at instant speed on their turn, but it stops only noncreature spells (not creature spells, so a combo that runs on a creature spell is not stopped, and not abilities). Blink and Sun Titan make it reusable.
- **Thalia, Heretic Cathar** ({2}{W}, 3/2 first strike): *Creatures and nonbasic lands your opponents control enter tapped.* It slows a combo that needs a creature to attack or tap the turn it arrives, or a nonbasic land for mana, and it does nothing against spells, artifacts or a combo that needs neither.
- **Instant removal:** Swords to Plowshares, Path to Exile, Deadly Rollick, Anguished Unmaking, and the flash creatures Solitude, Orcish Bowmasters (an X/1 piece) and The Wandering Emperor (-2 on a tapped creature).

Teferi's Protection and Flawless Maneuver do not stop an opponent's combo. Kambal costs an opponent 2 life for each noncreature spell they cast, which taxes tutors and rocks but does not stop a combo.

### Rules points not verified, and texts not read in full

The deck has not been simulated. These points are marked so that nobody relies on them:

- **UNVERIFIED: Delney and Skyclave Apparition's leave trigger.** Skyclave has power 2, so Delney should double its enter trigger (two exiles), which follows from the text. Whether its "leaves the battlefield" trigger also doubles, and what the second trigger does with two exiled cards, is not checked. Do not rely on it: do not blink or sacrifice Skyclave under Delney unless the exiled card was cheap.
- **UNVERIFIED: a doubler doubling the trigger for its own entry.** Delney, Gandalf and Elesh Norn enter the battlefield and are on it when the entering is checked, so they should double Yoshimaru's trigger for their own arrival (as the same kind of effect does for a permanent entering beside it). This is a rules reading, not checked against a ruling. The doubling table assumes it only where the text says so (a doubler already on the battlefield).
- **UNVERIFIED: the end-step triggers of Conjurer's Closet and Teleportation Circle.** The texts are read in full from the data (above). Two points are rules readings and not card text: that we choose the order of our own simultaneous triggers, and that a trigger aimed at a creature already returned by an earlier trigger has an illegal target (the creature is a new object). Do not rely on them without a check.
- **UNVERIFIED: Lotho and the Treasure interactions.** The texts are read from the card data: Lotho's trigger fires for any player's second spell each turn and is mandatory ("you lose 1 life and create a Treasure token"); Delney should double it (power 2, a triggered ability); a Treasure token is an artifact entering under our control, so Haliya's trigger fires for it and Gandalf and Elesh Norn each add a trigger. These are readings of the text, not checked against rulings or a simulation.
- **Texts cut off in the data:** Syr Vondam (the clause that begins "When Syr Vondam dies or is put into exile while its power is 4 or greater, destroy up to one target no" is cut; not used) and Sorin (the -X ability is cut after "Return target creature card with mana value X from your graveyard to the battlefield"; not used beyond that). The Eternal Wanderer's starting loyalty is not in the data.
- **Estimates:** the Yoshimaru totals, the 75% and 90% assumptions and the half of blink cards cast are judgement calls with no simulation behind them.
