# Why seat 1 has too little edge in Tier 6 (2026-10-24)

Branch `taxed-costs` at 4321b5e6: built-in knobs, adaptive AI, loose profile, 8200 games on seeds 500000+.

The sim gives seats 26.8 / 25.9 / 24.1 / 23.3% of decisive games. The target (EDHTop16, Apr-Sep 2026) is
32.5 / 26.9 / 22.7 / 17.9. Seat 4 / seat 1 is 0.87 in the sim against 0.55 in real play.

## Method

A temporary per-game line (since removed from `sim-cli/src/poolmode.rs`) recorded, for each game:
- the winner's seat;
- the game's length;
- each seat's first round with a ready line (`sp|ready`).

"First ready" is the earliest in game time (round × 4 + seat).

## Findings

1. **No skip draw.** The starting player draws on turn 1, as multiplayer rules say (`engine/turn.rs`).
2. **The first player to be ready wins 76% of the time, whatever their seat** (76 / 78 / 77 / 76%). Holding a
   ready line and interaction treat the seats alike, so they aren't what flattens the edge.
3. **The flattening is in who gets ready first: 28.4 / 25.2 / 23.9 / 22.5%.** Readiness hardly depends on turn
   order.
4. **Few players ever get a line ready.**
   - 72% of player-games never have one before the game ends.
   - On average 1.22 players per game get one (in the games where anyone does).
   - Ready rounds: mean 7.8, standard deviation 2.8.

   Most games are not a race: one player assembles a line and gets through or not, so going first rarely decides
   anything.
5. **The tournament clock explains about one point.** Real decisive games leave out the long games that hit time
   (24% draws). Sim seat shares among games won by round R:

   | won by round | share of games | seats |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 5 | 11% | 29.5 / 26.3 / 22.5 / 21.6 |
   | 6 | 27% | 29.3 / 25.2 / 23.7 / 21.8 |
   | 10 | 66% (about the real decisive share) | 27.6 / 25.6 / 24.2 / 22.6 |
   | any | 100% | 26.8 / 25.9 / 24.1 / 23.3 |

6. **Tightening the spread of ready times alone isn't enough.** I rescaled each player's ready round toward the
   mean and kept who gets ready at all. At a quarter of the spread, the first-ready seats are only 30.3 / 25.9 /
   23.4 / 20.5. The edge needs more of the table to reach a win attempt each game.
7. **The style knobs don't move it** (`t6-fit-2026-10-24.md`).

## Conclusion

The seat-1 shortfall and the speed shortfall (median win round 8 against 7) are one problem: the decks reach a
ready line too rarely and too late. Real cEDH has most of the table threatening a win by turns 3-5, so turn order
breaks the race.

Per deck (4000-game report, `taxed-costs-2026-10-24/lists2-cal.txt`):

| Deck | One tutor away | Ready | Median ready round |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kraum/Tymna | 52% | 43% | 7 |
| Kinnan | 44% | 18% | 7 |
| Rograkh/Silas | 39% | 33% | 7 |
| Sisay | 31% | 31% | 9 |
| Dack | 27% | 27% | 6 |
| Rograkh/Thrasios | 14% | 14% | 9 |

## Kinnan's gap (first look)

**Tutors aren't the problem.** While one tutor away, Kinnan casts its tutors (Finale of Devastation 225, Nature's
Rhythm 193, Invasion of Ikoria 171, Chord of Calling 160) and Kinnan himself (390).

**Removal is.** Kinnan takes the most removal at the table, and its lines all need him:

| From | Removals on Kinnan |
| --- | --- |
| Dack | 1949 |
| Sisay | 872 |
| Rograkh/Silas | 742 |
| Kraum/Tymna | 626 |
| Rograkh/Thrasios | 542 |

That's about 1.75 per seated game (4000-game report).

**Dack's volume is legal.** Dack resolves about 2.6 removals per game, four times any other deck. That comes from
Dack Fayden, Helping Hand: the revealed creatures enter under Dack's control, so Fiend Hunter's and All-Fates
Stalker's enter triggers are Dack's, before the opponents gain control of them. That matches Oracle and the deck's
first-flip plan.

So Kinnan's low conversion is a table effect: it is everyone's first removal target (threat value 8). It isn't a
tutor bug.

## Sisay, and the attempt success rate (later)

**Sisay's limit is mana.** A temporary per-turn diagnostic (2000 games) looked at Sisay's own turns:

| Round | Sisay on the battlefield | At power 5+ (Emiel) | Average mana |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 64% | 8% | 2.9 |
| 6 | 64% | 19% | 4.0 |
| 8 | 67% | 34% | 5.0 |

Every fetch costs {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} and the lines need two or three, so a line takes a turn or two of mana. Sisay is
already above the band, so this isn't a lever.

**Mox Diamond is not being held.** The report counted Mox Diamond as "held with the mana to cast" whenever its cost
was payable, even with no land to discard. That made 300+ card-turns per deck look like held fast mana.
`t6stats::mana_held` now requires a land in hand. A rule letting the deck discard its only land early changed
nothing (same deck shares to 0.1 points); that change was dropped.

**A first attempt's success by round** (8200 games):

| First go | Won |
| --- | --- |
| by round 4 | 76% |
| rounds 5-6 | 81% |
| rounds 7-8 | 83% |
| round 9+ | 86% |

By seat it's flat (83-84%). Early attempts fare worse, because opponents still hold full hands of free counters.

## Kraum/Tymna waits less (JD: option 1)

**The change.** The Oracle line now waits for protection only when:
- an opponent's stop chance is 0.5+ (was 0.3), or
- an opponent holds cards with two blue mana up (the spec's plain case, kept by its test).

The broad rule "any opponent with a card and a mana up" is gone. The Kraum and Esper Sentinel empty-library checks and
the late-game override are unchanged.

**Results** (8200 games; the report is 4000 games):
- Kraum/Tymna 27.8% → 29.3% (inside 18-32);
- ready games it never went in: 304 → 248 (of 1129);
- table: seat 1 26.8% (unchanged), spread 16.9 (was 17.2), median win round 8.

Files: `taxed-costs-2026-10-24/kwait2-8200.txt`, `kwait2-cal.txt`.

So one deck going more often doesn't make a race: the seat edge needs most of the table to reach lines earlier.
