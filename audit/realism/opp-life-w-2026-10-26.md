# Opponent-life weight in the lookahead (2026-10-26)

**Why.** `search::evaluate` scores a position as `strength(p) - 0.6 x strongest opponent - 0.4 x opponents' mean`. Life
counts 0.25 a point in `strength`. So a point of damage to the strongest opponent was worth 0.15, a point to any other
opponent about 0.03, and a point of your own life 0.25. The casual-speed trace (#151) named this as the deepest reason
attacks and deploys look poor to the lookahead.

**The knob.** `Search::opp_life_w`: an outside deck's evaluation subtracts `w x` each opponent's life, clamped to
0-60. JD's decks never use it (`ai::is_main`). **The default is 0, which leaves the evaluation unchanged** (JD,
2026-10-26). Set it with `[search] opp_life_w` in a `--knobs` file or with `SEARCH_OPP_LIFE_W` for A/B runs.

## Findings

- **It cuts the long tail.** At 0.3, games over 15 rounds drop by about a third in Tiers 0 and 2.
- **It barely moves the median win round:** by at most one round, against targets of 8-9. The rest of the slowness
  is board power (casual-speed: about 2.4 attackers with 7.6 power at round 6).
- **Tier 6 seat 1 doesn't move for real.** It reads 29.4% at 0.15 but 28.4% at 0.3, which is not monotone and is
  inside the noise.
- **Dominant decks gain a little.** The deck spread widens in most tiers: Giada goes from 31% to 34%, Kaalia from 29%
  to 32%.

Not turned on: the gain is mostly the long tail, it costs a little balance, and none of the gates moves.
