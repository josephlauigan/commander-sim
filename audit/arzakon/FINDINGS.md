Key `arzakon` since 2026-11-23; it was `kyodai` (and `thanos` until 2026-11-22). Commander Arzakon since 2026-11-23 (Kyodai, Soul of Kamigawa from 2026-11-22 to 2026-11-23, then in the 99 until 2026-11-23, when he left the list)

The deck's living list of strengths and weaknesses and what was tested.

**List:** `decklists/cali_commander/arzakon-artifact-combo.md`, built 2026-11-13 for Cali Commander (1v1, 30 life) from
the previous Thanos list, the Aragorn deck and cards taken from the other decks. Routes: Basalt Monolith + Rings of
Brighthearth, Staff of Domination + Faeburrow Elder, Tinker into Blightsteel Colossus (Sneak Attack as a second way to
put it in), and Thassa's Oracle with Demonic Consultation or Tainted Pact (Underworld Breach with Brain Freeze as a storm
line). **No sims yet.** The findings of the artifacts-and-Dragons list are in `FINDINGS-artifacts-and-dragons-list.md`,
those of the cEDH list in `FINDINGS-old-list.md`.

## 2026-11-24: Rhystic Study => Sunpearl Kirin (JD's choice, not simulated)
JD's decision: Sunpearl Kirin ({1}{W} 2/1 flier with flash, JD's own copy, from the spare list; model status Full) takes the slot of Rhystic Study ({2}{U} enchantment, a proxy in this deck, now out; it is also a proxy in the Ur-Dragon and Karn decks, and JD owns no copy). JD's reason: Rhystic Study triggers on one opponent here and is stronger in four-player pods. Not simulated (JD's choice). A package change, not slot-for-slot: a blue enchantment draw engine out, a white flash creature that returns a permanent in.
- **Job lost: a draw engine that watches one opponent.** Rhystic Study drew a card whenever the opponent cast a spell unless they paid {1}. The plan named it as an engine, in a `[[wish]]` row (with The One Ring, turn 3 or earlier) and in a `[[prio]]` row. Recorded: opponents removed it 0.04 times a game and, in the 2026-11-23 baseline (below: the Kyodai-commander list in a 4-player, 40-life t5 pod, not Cali and not this list), it was cast in 22.1% of games and won 23.8% of the games it was cast in against the deck's 15.1% (+8.7, among the ten highest; correlation only: a cast engine is also a sign of a game that lasted). There is no figure for it at the Cali format.
- **Job gained: a cheap flash creature that saves or replays a permanent.** When Sunpearl Kirin enters, return up to one other target nonland permanent you control to its owner's hand; if it was a token, draw a card. At instant speed, in response to a spell or ability that targets one of the deck's permanents, it returns that permanent and the removal has no target left (it does nothing against a counterspell on a combo spell or a removal spell that does not target). It can also return Dockside Extortionist or Meteor Golem to cast it again for another enter effect, and returning a token draws a card. The Kirin is a white permanent: it is one more white permanent toward Faeburrow Elder's colours without Leyline of the Guildpact (the Elder, Grand Abolisher and the Kirin are white; the count without Leyline stays at four colours and nothing is black).
- **Counts after (before):** creatures 16 (15); enchantments 4 (5); instants 23 (23); artifacts 20 (20), 24 with the artifact creatures (24); nonland cards at mana value 2: 20 (19), at 3: 13 (14); average mana value of the 72 nonland cards besides the commander 2.36 (2.38), 170 over 72 (171); Game Changers 16 (17); land sources unchanged. One blue card fewer, one white card more.
- **Plan file (`data/plans/arzakon.toml`), lines changed:** the header comment (the 2026-11-24 note and the engine list in the intro); `engines` (line 17, now The One Ring, Mystic Remora, Urza, Crucible); the `[[wish]]` row `["The One Ring", "Rhystic Study"]` (turn<=3, score 80) now `["The One Ring"]`; the `[[prio]]` row `["Rhystic Study", "The One Ring", "Mystic Remora"]` (turn<=6, value 78, else 62) now `["The One Ring", "Mystic Remora"]` with the same values; `[sets] engines` (line 144, the mulligan's engine set). Every other card and value is kept, and the Kirin has no row. This changes the AI's play for this deck: The One Ring and Mystic Remora keep their priorities, and a hand whose only engine was Rhystic Study no longer counts it as one for the keep rule.
- **Model status and limits:** Sunpearl Kirin is Full (`data/card_overrides.toml`, `impls/yoshimaru_p6.rs`). The AI returns the permanent whose enter effect is worth replaying most (at least 2) or a token worth less than the card it draws, never a commander or a Treasure, Food, Clue or Blood token; the AI has no rule that flashes it in against removal (it is not in the protector table), so the "save a combo piece" job is a person's. The Kirin's trigger is checked against its own colours as the engine does for every targeted trigger.
- **To check in the first run:** how often the Kirin is cast, on which turn and what it returns (the log line `Sunpearl Kirin returns ...`); whether the AI ever returns Dockside Extortionist or Meteor Golem; and whether the removal of a combo piece is more often answered by a person than by the AI.

## 2026-11-24: Teferi's Protection relabelled Approximate (JD's waiver); the phasing and Rings work not built
Two Opus design reviews (documents/design-teferis-protection-phasing.md and documents/design-ai-rings-copies.md) looked at the two open tickets; JD chose not to build either and to label Teferi's Protection Approximate.
- **Teferi's Protection** was marked Full, but the engine phases out only the caster's nonland permanents. Its lands are only tapped, its Treasure, Food, Clue and Blood counters stay spendable, its floating mana is emptied (the rules keep it), an opponent's Aura or Equipment on its permanents does not phase out, and the protection ends at upkeep, not as the turn begins. The card note and `audit/GAME_CHANGERS.md` now say so, and the status is **Approximate**. JD waived the Full requirement (2026-11-24): `cardgen audit --mine` now lists it for this deck (and for any list that plays it) in addition to Yoshimaru's Delney, which was already Approximate. Dockside Extortionist works around it by counting an opponent with `life_locked` and `ring_prot` as 0 (dormant today, since this is the only Dockside deck and it never faces a deck that plays the card; Flare of Fortitude with The One Ring would also read as 0).
- **What this does to the numbers.** Every recorded result in which a list played the card was measured with the approximation; its direction and size are unmeasured. That covers this deck (no sims of the current list yet), Sheoldred (t3 against Aurelia, t4 against Edgar Markov) and Yoshimaru (t4 against Edgar Markov). It also covers the Ur-Dragon (t5) and Yoshimaru (t4, t5) lists from before PR #394, which played the card themselves: their results up to 2026-11-23 and the old arm of the 2026-11-24 paired tests (`audit/arzakon-package-paired-2026-11-24/`). The largest effect Opus found is the caster's lands staying tapped under Winter Orb, Static Orb and Back to Basics (this deck against urza-mono-blue-artifacts at t5). In the other direction, the caster can still spend Treasure, Clue and Blood during the window. Because its lands are only tapped, other players' Exotic Orchard and Fellwar Stone still read their colours, and the caster's own Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth still makes every land a Swamp (Edgar Markov's, in every t4 pod). Today the card is in this deck and three pool decks: kaalia-mardu-creature-cheat (t2), aurelia-boros-extra-combats (t3) and edgar-markov-mardu-vampires (t4).
- **Gaps Opus found on the way, not built and not ticketed** (JD has not decided): artifact wipes never destroy Treasure, Clue, Food or Blood counters or artifact lands (`engine/removal.rs`); the AI's player targeting ignores protection from everything in at least Liliana of the Veil's -6; The One Ring's lock ends at upkeep, not at turn start; the look-ahead values phased permanents at 0.
- **Rings of Brighthearth** is unchanged: the AI never copies loyalty abilities with it (limit 1 below stands). Opus's guess for what it would add is 0 to 2 points for this deck, unmeasured; building it would also move every t5 baseline through the Urza pool deck.

## 2026-11-23: commander Kyodai, Soul of Kamigawa => Arzakon (JD's choice, not simulated)
Arzakon ({W}{U}{B}{R}{G}, a legendary planeswalker, loyalty 4; +2: 3 damage to any other target; -3: a Black Lotus token; -7: each player shuffles hand and graveyard into the library and draws seven; "can be your commander") becomes the commander, and Kyodai moves into the 99 (the pair below). Nothing was simulated: no sim exists for this commander, and the 4-player baseline below is for the old list. Opus's consult is `/tmp/handoff/opus-arzakon-design.md`.
**What it gives:** a repeatable 3-damage ping that kills Collector Ouphe, mana dorks and Ragavan and is a ten-turn clock at 30 life; a -3 that returns three mana of one colour and an artifact (Mox Opal's third artifact, Tinker fodder, a Tezzeret loyalty counter); a -7 wheel when the table's draw punishers are absent. A Rings of Brighthearth at a real table copies loyalty abilities (a copied -3 is two Lotus tokens for {2}, a copied +2 is 6 damage). **What it costs:** the cast gets harder (all five colours; the colourless rocks that paid for Kyodai pay nothing toward Arzakon; expect turn 4 to 5, not turn 2 to 3; Leyline of the Guildpact turns every land any colour and is cast in 32.0% of the games it is drawn, before); it deals no commander damage and has no flying clock (commander damage was 4.4% of eliminations in the baseline below); the removal pressure Kyodai drew (removed 0.24 times a game, before) moves onto a 4-loyalty walker, which is easy to attack down in a 1v1; Deflecting Swat, Akroma's Will and Jeska's Will need a commander on the battlefield, so they work only while Arzakon is out (they left the deck in the pair below). The Black Lotus token is legal under the Cali ban list because a token is not a card in the deck (the ban list is checked against deck lists only).
**Plan:** `data/plans/arzakon.toml` `[commander] prio = 74, turn = 3`, provisional (Opus: a first cast is 8.9 with the first-cast bonus, above the engines at 7.8 and the turn-4 fast mana at 8.6; a recast after it dies is about 6.1, below the engines). A `[[prio]]` row of 60 for Kyodai in the 99 (removed 2026-11-23 when he left the list).
**Model status:** Arzakon is Full (the exported DSL record was Partial; it is hand-written now: a hand-written walker with the AI rules below, a Black Lotus token through the Garth path, the Timetwister wheel as a shared helper). Kyodai, Karn, Voltaic Key and Pithing Needle are Full.
**Limits JD accepted 2026-11-23** (Opus's list, each in one line):
1. Rings of Brighthearth does not copy Arzakon's loyalty abilities for the AI (the AI's Rings copies only inside the Basalt loop); a person's Rings does. JD accepted 2026-11-23.
2. Planeswalker attacks are assigned after blockers (engine-wide), so the AI kills Arzakon more efficiently than a real table, and a defender cannot block "for" Arzakon. JD accepted 2026-11-23.
3. Opponents' generic burn never aims at planeswalkers (engine-wide), which favours the deck. JD accepted 2026-11-23.
4. Damage to a noncreature planeswalker skips damage prevention and ward (engine-wide); it touches the +2 at a warded or protected planeswalker (none in t5 or t6) and damage dealt to Arzakon. JD accepted 2026-11-23.
5. Opponents over-value Arzakon as a threat: the symmetric -7 is read as a game-ending ultimate, so the table focuses attacks and removal on it more than a human would (engine-wide). JD accepted 2026-11-23.
6. The Black Lotus token is spent like Lotus Petal: it goes after single-colour sources, nothing saves it for the combo turn, and Tinker's sacrifice may pick it. JD accepted 2026-11-23.
7. The AI rules for the +2 target, when to -3 and when to -7, and the commander priority 74 (turn 3), are provisional heuristics that no sim has tuned. JD accepted 2026-11-23.
8. Built, so not limits: the +2 can target a battle (the AI never aims it at one; a person can), and a person is asked for the +2 target. JD decided 2026-11-23.
9. Deck consequences, not engine limits: no commander damage and no flying clock; Swat, Jeska's Will and Akroma's Will work only while Arzakon, a five-colour sorcery-speed 5-drop planeswalker, is on the battlefield (Kyodai in the 99 no longer counts). JD accepted 2026-11-23.
10. Engine drift to disclose before the next baselines: the look-ahead fix that stops it counting a Black Lotus token as three mana every turn (`ai/search.rs`) changes Garth One-Eye's look-ahead in the urdragon deck, and the wheel helper's fix that sends a commander in hand to the command zone changes Timetwister when a commander is held (no t5, t6 or JD deck is likely to hit it). JD accepted 2026-11-23.
11. The old Kyodai baseline (4-player, 40 life, Kyodai as commander, the section below) does not carry over to this commander or to the Cali format. JD accepted 2026-11-23.
**Queued:** a commander-priority sweep (70, 74 and 80 in one paired Cali run) runs after this change merges, because the Cali AI profile (C4) is on main now. A paired test of a commander change is `--swap-commander OLD=>NEW` (built in this change).

## Baseline 2026-11-23 (main f1ffd896, Stage 3; look-ahead, loose, 1,500 games, seed 500000; the Kyodai-commander list, before the changes above)
**t5: 15.1%** (about ±1.8 at 95%, binomial arithmetic), 4-player baseline 25%, average rounds survived 8.2. Raw (`--analyze`): `audit/stage3-baseline-2026-11-23/kyodai-analyze.txt`.
This is the first simulation of this list (the intro above, written before it, still says "No sims yet"). It is a baseline from the new engine (exact X and alternative-cost pricing, hybrid mana, the
Stage 2 filters); no older number in this file is for this list or build, so none is comparable. It is also a 4-player pod at 40 life, not Cali (1v1, 30 life: see "What the simulator cannot do yet"),
so it measures the list against the t5 table, not at the Cali format.

- **How it wins:** combo 84.1% of wins, damage 15.9%. It eliminates 0.44 opponents a game: combo 81.3%, combat 12.5%, commander damage 4.4%, triggers 1.4%, decked 0.5%. Damage dealt 8.6 a game, all combat.
- **How it loses:** eliminated in 84.9% of games, on average in round 8.2. Kinnan combo 16.6%, Urza combo 13.9%, Miirym combat 12.1%, Zur combo 8.4%, Yawgmoth combo 7.7%, Miirym triggers 6.3%, Kinnan combat 4.7%,
  Yawgmoth drain 3.4%.

What the numbers say (correlation, not causation):
- **It wins only when the plan goes online.** The plan is online in 14.9% of games and the deck wins 15.1%; combo is 84.1% of wins, about 12.7% of games (arithmetic), and damage 15.9% of wins, about 2.4%.
- **The plan comes late.** Online by T8 in 12% of games and by T4 in 1%, while the deck is eliminated on average in round 8.2 and in 84.9% of games. The Kinnan and Urza combos eliminate it in 30.5% of games (16.6% plus 13.9%), against about
  12.7% of games it wins by its own combo. Why the plan is slow is not in the output (mana, pieces, interaction): unmeasured.
- **The combo pieces are cast rarely and some sit in hand.** Thassa's Oracle wins 79.6% of the games it is cast in but is cast in 7.2% of games, and in only 26.9% of the games it is drawn; Tainted Pact 28.1%,
  Brain Freeze 2.2%, Blightsteel Colossus 13.1%. The intro above has Blightsteel coming in through Tinker or Sneak Attack and the Oracle needing Consultation or Pact, so low cast rates may be missing partners rather than the AI holding
  the card; the output does not separate them: unmeasured. The Breach line is live in 3.3% of games and wins 0.3%.
- **The common route is Tinker or Monolith plus Rings.** Tinker (cast in 10.5% of games, wins 32.9%, +17.8), Rings of Brighthearth (29.5%, 29.2%) and Basalt Monolith (34.8%, 26.2%) are the highest after the Oracle.
  Kyodai is cast in 84.7% of games at a win rate equal to the deck's (15.0%, -0.0) and is removed 0.24 times a game, the most of any card.
- **The cheap mana cards show no lift.** Dark Ritual 12.3% (-2.8), Birds of Paradise 12.8% (-2.3), Mox Opal 14.4%, Mana Vault 14.9% when cast, all at or below the deck's 15.1%; the output's own note calls
  low numbers on cheap cards cast early the most telling. Whether mana is the bottleneck is unmeasured.

**Kyodai** (`impls/kyodai.rs`): the enter trigger is a stack trigger (the table can respond; Elesh Norn blanks it, Panharmonicon doubles it): another target permanent that is
