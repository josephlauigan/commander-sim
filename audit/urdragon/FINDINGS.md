# The Ur-Dragon (key `urdragon`): findings

The deck's living list of strengths and weaknesses and what was tested.

**List:** `decklists/JD/urdragon-five-colour-dragons.md`, built 2026-11-13: an optimised high-bracket-4 Dragons list with
proxies allowed, reworked 2026-11-14 (24 Dragons besides the commander, 25 with Morophon since 2026-11-23 (25 and 26 from 2026-11-23), ten original duals, seven fetches, five shocks; the Garth One-Eye + Spark Double + Twinflame +
Doubling Season combo). **Tiers:** t5 (`data/my_decks.toml`). **No sims yet.** The findings of the
retired Ur-Dragon decks are in `audit/retired/urdragon/`.

## 2026-11-23: Hellkite Courser, Klauth, Unrivaled Ancient => Talisman of Hierarchy, Kyodai, Soul of Kamigawa (JD's choice, not simulated)
Two pairs after the package below; none simulated. Both cuts were proxies (Courser and Klauth); both cards in are owned (Talisman of Hierarchy and Kyodai come from the Arzakon deck). JD approved the swaps 2026-11-23.
- **Hellkite Courser => Talisman of Hierarchy.** Package-level, not slot-for-slot (a six-mana Dragon out, a two-mana rock in). Job lost: the bridge from the package below (a {4}{R}{R} 6/5 flying Dragon that put The Ur-Dragon onto the battlefield with haste for one attack at five mana, three with Urza's Incubator), and a Dragon body. Job gained: a turn-2 rock that taps for {C}, or {W} or {B} at 1 damage, to make white and black, the thinnest colours in the list, and to cast a turn-3 four-drop; it is an artifact, so an artifact wipe kills it. The commander still comes out only through its own nine mana (seven with Incubator), so this pair gives up the one speed card that skipped the cost.
- **Klauth, Unrivaled Ancient => Kyodai, Soul of Kamigawa.** Package-level (a seven-mana Dragon out, a four-mana flash Dragon in). Klauth was a proxy. Job lost: a seven-drop that The Ur-Dragon drops in free from the new hand (the deck keeps Tiamat, Old Gnawbone, Smaug the Impenetrable, Morophon and Utvara Hellkite for that), and Klauth's mana from combat damage (Old Gnawbone still makes Treasures from damage). Job gained: a flash 3/3 flier at {3}{W} ({2}{W} with Eminence, {W} with Eminence and Urza's Incubator naming Dragon: both apply to a Dragon), whose enter trigger gives another target permanent indestructible while he stays, which answers a destroy wipe for one permanent (the deck's wipe weakness), and he triggers Terror of the Peaks, Scourge of Valkas and Dragon Tempest as a Dragon entering. The {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} pump is +5/+5 for all five colours at once. Costs: it answers one permanent, not the board, and not exile, bounce, -X/-X or counterspells; he is white, and a white source is a thin colour here.
**Counts after (before):** Dragons 24 besides the commander (25), 25 with Morophon (26); creatures 33 (34) besides the commander; artifacts 9 (8); enchantments 6 (6); instants 9 (9); sorceries 9 (9); lands 33 (33); Game Changers 10 (10); cards with flash: 1 (0), Kyodai; nonland cards at mana value 2: 9 (8), at 4: 9 (8), at 6: 8 (9), at 7: 4 (5); average mana value of the 66 nonland cards besides the commander 3.89 (4.00).

Korvold was removed 0.04 times a game (before). With a commander on the battlefield both modes work: R for each card in an opponent's hand and the top three cards playable this turn.

## Baseline 2026-11-23 (main f1ffd896, Stage 3; look-ahead, loose, 1,500 games, seed 500000)
**t5: 27.6%** (about ±2.3 at 95%, binomial arithmetic), 4-player baseline 25%, average rounds survived 8.5. Raw (`--analyze`): `audit/stage3-baseline-2026-11-23/urdragon-analyze.txt`.
This is the first simulation of this list (the intro above, written before it, still says "No sims yet"). It is a baseline from the new engine (exact X and alternative-cost pricing, hybrid mana, the
Stage 2 filters); no older number in this file is for this list or build, so none is comparable.

- **How it wins:** damage 96.6% of wins, alt 2.9%, timeout 0.5%. It eliminates 0.82 opponents a game: combat 56.7%, triggers 39.9%, burn 2.2%, commander damage 1.0%. Damage dealt 42.4 a game: combat 30.3 (72%),
  triggers 11.7 (28%), burn 0.3.
- **How it loses:** eliminated in 72.3% of games, on average in round 7.9. Urza combo 13.7%, Kinnan combo 13.1%, Miirym combat 9.1% and triggers 6.9%, Zur combo 8.9%, Yawgmoth combo 6.9% and drain 2.5%,
  Kinnan combat 2.9%.
- **Plan timing: unmeasured.** The plan line prints blank and online is 0% at every turn and 0.0% at all, so the output says nothing about this deck's plan. The Ur-Dragon resolves in only 45.6% of games, median
  turn 7.0 (mean 7.4); by T4 2%, T5 8%, T6 18%, T7 27%, T8 34%.
- **Often stuck in hand** (cast in under half the games drawn): Teferi's Protection 24.0% (reactive), Twinflame 25.0%, Triple Triad 31.1%, Doubling Season 32.1%, Garth One-Eye 34.4%, Scion of the Ur-Dragon 35.2%,
  Nibelheim Aflame 35.3%, Austere Command 37.1%, Decimate 39.4%, Steel Hellkite 41.0%, Dragonlord Silumgar 41.7%, Summon: Bahamut 42.1%.
- **Most removed by opponents (per game):** The Ur-Dragon 0.15, Terror of the Peaks 0.06, Dragonlord Dromoka 0.04, Korvold 0.04, Goldspan Dragon, Scourge of Valkas, Hellkite Tyrant, Summon: Bahamut 0.03 each.

What the numbers say (correlation, not causation):
- **Most wins come after The Ur-Dragon is cast.** It is cast in 46.5% of games and wins 52.9% of them (+25.3): about 24.6% of games, roughly 89% of the 27.6% (arithmetic from those two figures). In the other
  53.5% of games the deck wins about 5.6%. Why it is not cast in those games (mana, removal, the hand) is not in the output: unmeasured.
- **The deck is slow to its commander.** Median turn 7.0, and by T6 only 18%: the output shows no ramp or mana-by-turn figure, so how much of this is the mana base or the top-heavy curve (the Open questions below
  ask about both) is unmeasured.
- **The big Dragons win when they land and some sit in hand.** Tiamat 69.7% (cast in 13.9% of games), Summon: Bahamut 63.8% (9.9%), Utvara Hellkite 61.7% (11.1%), Scion of the Ur-Dragon 57.5% (7.5%), Morophon 56.8%;
  Summon: Bahamut is cast in 42.1% of the games drawn, Steel Hellkite 41.0%, Dragonlord Silumgar 41.7%, Scion 35.2%. Expensive cards are cast in games that are already going well, so this does not show that
  they help.
- **The Garth line is mostly held.** Garth One-Eye is cast in 34.4% of the games it is drawn, Doubling Season 32.1%, Twinflame 25.0%. The Twinflame hold while the line is alive is by design (the card-code fixes
  below); how often the line fired, or won, is not in the output: unmeasured (alt wins are 2.9% of wins).
- **The lowest-ranked cards are not low.** All ten lowest win rates when cast are above the deck's 27.6% (+4.8 to +8.9: Chaos Warp, Rivaz, Rhythm of the Wild 33.7%, Mana Vault, Fierce Guardianship, Swords,
  Path, Three Visits, The One Ring, Llanowar Elves), so the list shows no card cast with a win rate below the deck's (a cast card is also a sign of a game that lasted). Rhythm of the Wild (the 11-20 swap) is cast in 11.3% of games; Nibelheim Aflame (the 11-22 swap) is cast in 35.3% of the
  games drawn.

## 2026-11-22: Cyclonic Rift => Nibelheim Aflame (JD's choice, not simulated)
Nibelheim Aflame ({2}{R}{R} sorcery, flashback {5}{R}{R}; model status Full; owned) takes Cyclonic Rift's slot (a proxy). It is
not slot-for-slot: Rift was a {1}{U} instant that, overloaded at {6}{U}, bounced every nonland permanent the opponents control
and left yours alone. Nibelheim Aflame lets a creature you control deal damage equal to its power to each other creature, yours
included, so it kills the deck's own Dragons whenever their toughness is at or below that power; the chosen creature takes
nothing. Its flashback discards the hand and draws four, a late refill Rift never had. What it costs: an instant-speed one-sided
reset, a Game Changer (11 to 10, still Bracket 4), Rift's two-mana mode (Nibelheim costs four; both reach seven mana in their late modes), and a blue card (Rift needed {U}; the
new card needs {R}{R}). Counts after: instants 9 (10), sorceries 8 (7), wipes Crux of Fate, Austere Command, Nibelheim Aflame and
Steel Hellkite; the Dragon count and the lands are unchanged. The two Dragon-sparing wipes (Crux of Fate's non-Dragon mode, Austere
Command's mana-value mode) stay. The plan file does not list wipes in `[[prio]]`, so the generic wipe logic (your own losses count
1.2x) decides when it is cast; the engine (`rust/crates/sim-core/src/engine/removal.rs`) picks the caster's highest-power creature as the chosen one, so it will usually be The Ur-Dragon or another large Dragon, and the deck's other Dragons and creatures die. Not measured. To check in the first run: how often it
is cast and what it kills of the deck's own board, how often the flashback is cast, and what the loss of the Rift reset costs
against wipes and fast combos.

## 2026-11-15: Talrand, Sky Summoner => Triple Triad (JD's choice, not simulated)
Triple Triad ({3}{R}{R}{R}, model status Full: `data/card_overrides.toml`, tests in
`rust/crates/sim-core/tests/cards_seph_triad.rs`) takes Talrand's slot. Why: at your upkeep each player exiles their top
card and you play your own card and every other exiled card of lower mana value free. About a third of the 99 are lands (no
free spell, but you may play one as your land drop) and 29 are Dragons, mostly costing 5 to 7, so a flip is a free Dragon
about three times in ten, and the higher your flip, the more of the opponents' exiled cards qualify. Talrand triggers on only seventeen instants and sorceries, five of them early tutors and
ramp, his Drakes are not Dragons (no Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest, Ganax, Utvara or Ur-Dragon draw), and he costs
{U}{U} on the thinnest colour. What it costs: a six-mana enchantment that needs {R}{R}{R} (20 red sources) and does nothing
the turn it lands, and it does not help against a wipe. Not measured. An Opus consult ranked Heroic Intervention first for
this slot (it answers the documented weakness, a wipe after the Dragons are committed; JD had cut it on 11/14); JD chose
Triple Triad. The plan file now has a priority row for Triple Triad (62 from six lands and mana permanents, no else), because
Sauron's 11/01 findings record that the generic AI once never cast it. Still to check in the first run: Triple Triad's cast and
stuck-in-hand numbers, and what it flips.

## What the simulator cannot do yet

- **Plan file:** `data/plans/urdragon.toml` (written 2026-11-14, provisional: from the pilot guide, not a paired test). Its header lists what a plan file cannot say.
- **Card-code fixes (tests in `tests/ai_urdragon_garth_line.rs`):** The Ur-Dragon's free drop no longer puts Tiamat onto the battlefield (her search is "if you cast it"; she stays in hand to be cast). Twinflame is held while the Garth line is alive (Garth, Spark Double and Doubling Season each in hand, library or on the battlefield), so the combo is its only play once the line is ready. The hold is released when a piece is dead, when the table's visible attack can kill the seat, or when Twinflame's copies and the ready fliers are lethal on one opponent in the first main phase and the engine, playing it out on a copy of the game, then makes the kill. That lethal estimate counts what the resolution really makes (Doubling Season's extra copies, bounded by the token cap; no copy of a creature with protection from red), the damage that can reach the opponent (combat damage only if the opponent has no untapped flier or reach blocker, no attack cap is out anywhere and the opponent's attacks are not taxed; creatures locked by Arrest-style Auras, creatures whose damage is prevented and, in a fog turn, all combat damage are left out), and Terror of the Peaks' triggers, replayed one at a time the way the engine aims them (an opponent the trigger can kill takes it first, else a creature the engine would remove, else the most threatening opponent, with each opponent's life updated after every trigger). An opponent is counted only when nothing reduces or stops the damage: The One Ring protection, Glacial Chasm, a locked life total, Gisela's halving, Angel's Grace, Phyrexian Unlife, Herald of Eternal Dawn, Serra's emblem and Pariah all make it uncountable; a hexproof opponent takes no Terror damage in the count. Spark Double copies Garth when Doubling Season (in hand or on the battlefield) and Twinflame (in hand only: a Twinflame on the battlefield is spent) are there (or a Demonic or Vampiric Tutor can fetch the missing one), else the best creature as before; only the seat with the key `urdragon` takes the Garth branches. Two corrections to the fair Twinflame itself apply to every seat (it is in no other registered deck): its copies are doubled by Doubling Season (one `repl::tokens` count per target, up to the token cap), and a creature with protection from red is skipped as a target. Not simulated yet: the effect on the kill turn and the share of wins by the Garth route is for the first goldfish and tier-5 runs.
- **Still open:** a Spark Double cast, or free-dropped by The Ur-Dragon, before Garth is on the battlefield copies something else and kills the line; holding Spark Double until Garth lands is a separate change to the cast logic. Twinflame also stays held for as long as the pieces are in the library, which is most of the game: whether that hold should last that long is an open policy question, not a measured result. A permanent that locks the line (an opponent's Rule of Law) does not release the hold either: it is zone-only.
- **Limits of the lethal release (recorded behaviour, not simulated):**
  - How it is checked: the estimate below must find an opponent it can kill, and then the engine must make the kill. Twinflame is cast on a copy of the game that knows only what the Ur-Dragon seat knows (each opponent's hand goes back into its library and the libraries are shuffled; the exiled cards an opponent may play, which the engine holds in its hand, stay, since they are known), and the copy attacks each opponent the estimate found with everything, three times on different random numbers, and every attack must kill. Twinflame is released only if an opponent leaves the game after the cast or after one of those attacks, and the caster does not. Everything the engine does on the visible board between the cast and the end of that combat is followed as the engine plays it (Kor Haven, Opposition, Whipcorder, Ballista Squad, an animated Mishra's Factory, Painful Quandary on the caster, paying for Twinflame with a counted Dragon). The copy uses its own random numbers and the real game's state is not changed (a cache keyed by the battlefield and diagnostic counters can fill, as in the look-ahead). Inside the AI's own look-ahead copies the estimate decides alone.
  - L1, the estimate counts each Terror of the Peaks trigger from the Terrors already on the battlefield. When Twinflame copies a Terror and Doubling Season makes two tokens, the second token's entry also triggers the first token, which the estimate leaves out. The error only delays the release, never spends it early.
  - L2, the release reads only what is visible when Twinflame is cast. A fog, Teferi's Protection, Angel's Grace, Tishana's Tidebinder, a counterspell or instant-speed removal that an opponent casts from the hand can still stop the kill; that is true of any attack the AI makes. In the check, a card an opponent draws during the line (Nezahal, Rhystic Study) is a random card from its library, and an opponent's hand is empty, so an effect that counts the cards in a hand reads 0 there. The caster's own library is shuffled in the check too (except the cards it knows are on top), so a card it draws during the line is a random card as well: The Ur-Dragon's attack trigger draws one card per attacking Dragon and puts the best permanent from the hand onto the battlefield, and a lucky draw (Fiery Emancipation, Scourge of Valkas) can prove a kill the real draws do not give. The release then also commits the all-out attack (L4). The three attacks share that one shuffled order, so they vary the blocks, not the draws. The order Terror's triggers resolve in when an opponent can answer them is not modelled either (the replay assumes the order they were put on). A foretold card is treated as known in the check (the engine holds it in the hand like an exiled card the opponent may play), and in practice mode a person's seat is played there by the AI.
  - L3, the release does not count combat damage to an opponent whose attacks are taxed (Propaganda, Ghostly Prison), or while any attack cap is out (Angelic Arbiter, Sandwurm Convergence, Crawlspace, Silent Arbiter). It also does not treat as killable an opponent whose damage is halved, floored or not lethal (Gisela, Angel's Grace, Phyrexian Unlife, the Serra emblem) or whose life can't change. In those states, or while an opponent's Blind Obedience-style effect makes the copies enter tapped (Blind Obedience, Thalia: a tapped copy can't attack, but its entry still triggers Terror), Twinflame stays held unless Terror's triggers alone are lethal. The AI does not keep mana back for an attack tax, so the mana left after Twinflame is not counted on. Twinflame also stays held, even when Terror's triggers alone would be lethal, while an opponent controls a permanent that triggers on the line itself: a creature entering (Soul Warden, Soul's Attendant, Auriok Champion, Authority of the Consuls, Suture Priest, Intruder Alarm while it has its abilities), an opponent casting a spell (Sunscorch Regent, Arasta of the Endless Web), a player attacking (Aurelia, the Law Above), a player losing life (Exquisite Blood, Bloodthirsty Conqueror) or the start of a combat (Koma, Cosmos Serpent, Sting, the Glinting Dagger). It does not count combat damage to an opponent with a block or defence effect on the battlefield (Spore Frog, Mirage Mirror, Yawgmoth, Brimaz). These holds are the estimate's own and stay even where the engine would kill: Yawgmoth's counters can't touch 8-toughness Dragons and Nezahal only draws a card, but while Yawgmoth is out no combat-only kill of its controller is released, and while Nezahal or Koma is out no release happens at the table. They are pinned by tests and are a decision for JD (release more, once the check has been through its own adversarial round).
  - L4, the attack after the release. The check attacks one opponent with everything, and the AI now takes that attack: once Twinflame's copies have entered after a release the check proved, the seat's first combat that turn attacks with everything the opponent the check attacked (the first in seat order whose three attacks each made an opponent leave the game). The proof is that some opponent leaves the game, usually that one: an attack trigger (Drakuseth, Maw of Flames) can kill another while the attacked one survives, and attacking the one who died would waste the attack. The commitment applies in every AI mode (look-ahead, adaptive, ISMCTS). The plan is keyed to the seat and its turn count (an extra turn in the same round does not inherit it), is used once and is cleared after the combat (a look-ahead copy that resumes after combat keeps it unused, and the key drops it at the next combat that asks). It is checked again on the real board when the attack is chosen, with the lethal estimate (the copies that entered count as ready fliers, Terror's triggers have already been dealt): the plan is dropped, and the combat chooses as before, when the opponent has left the game, or when the estimate no longer finds it killable and is lower than it was right after the copies entered (life gained, the copies gone or tapped, a fog, a new flying or reach blocker). The comparison starts once the copies have entered: a target that left the battlefield during the cast is caught (the plan then needs the estimate to find the opponent killable now). That includes a target the check's copy lost the same way: an opponent's cast trigger (Kaervek the Merciless) is played in the check too, so its loss is already in the proof, yet the plan is dropped when the estimate undercounts what is left (double strike, a damage doubler), a conservative drop. In the other direction, life gained in response, or fewer copies per target (Doubling Season removed in response), is not caught when the estimate is already below lethal after the cast. AI opponents answer a spell only with counters and the stack responses, and their cast triggers are played in the check, so the uncaught cases concern a person's seat in practice mode. An estimate that undercounts the proven attack on an unchanged board (double strike, a damage doubler) keeps it. The re-check reads only what the estimate reads: a defence only the engine check plays (Kor Haven, Opposition, Whipcorder, Ballista Squad, an animated Mishra's Factory) that becomes usable between the cast and the attack is not caught (no engine path that does this was found), and it measures the attacked opponent, so life that opponent gains drops the plan even when the proven kill was another opponent's. Only the first combat of the turn is committed; later combats choose as before and the copies stay for them. The cast carries a plan only when it is a release proven by an attack: a cast the cast's own triggers completed (Terror of the Peaks kills), a main-phase-2 cast, a cast made because the Garth line is dead or the seat is in danger, a cast inside a look-ahead copy, another seat's Twinflame, a person's seat and a countered cast carry none. What remains open: the check's opponents hold no cards, but in the real game they can act from their hands after the attack is declared (a fog, a flash blocker, instant-speed removal on an attacker, a response from `attack_response`), and the forced all-out attack then leaves everything tapped, with ground creatures sent as well (the plan's stay-home rules still apply) and the crack-back that invites. The look-ahead used to hedge against that with dealt hands; the commitment does not. Before combat the AI may also cast other spells that the check does not play (a spell that triggers Painful Quandary or an opponent's Arasta); the re-check catches the changes it can see. Only a paired tier-5 run shows what the commitment costs; the goldfish gate cannot, because goldfish opponents never interact or attack back.
  - L7, an opponent's chump block is a random choice in the engine (about 99.97% likely against a lethal attack); the check plays each attack three times on different random numbers (the same shuffled libraries, see L2) and needs every one to kill, so a block the engine nearly always makes is not missed. In the real game an opponent's blocks can instead be chosen by its block search, which with several attackers may chump a different one than the engine's heuristic does. The estimate already holds against any untapped flier or reach creature, so this only concerns a blocker that appears in the combat itself (an animated Mishra's Factory).
  - L8, where the engine departs from the rules, the check follows the engine: Twinflame's own cast path ignores Chalice of the Void, Opposition still taps after losing its abilities, an Intruder Alarm that lost its abilities still stops creatures untapping in the untap step, and compiled triggers ignore lost abilities. These are separate engine issues, not fixed here.
  - L9, the look-ahead's view of the cast. The commitment is never set inside a look-ahead copy, so the main phase's look-ahead still scores the Twinflame option with the heuristic attack, not the committed all-out attack the real game will play. Scoring the option under the committed attack is a separate change.
  - No longer limits (L5 printed power, L6 card-code triggers): the copies' printed power and opponents' triggers written in card code can't spend Twinflame wrongly any more. The estimate still reads them as before, but the check plays the real ones, so an overcount only holds the release.

  Niv-Mizzet and Utvara Hellkite are compiled from Oracle text and covered by `tests/cards_urdragon_swaps.rs` (Utvara

## Open questions

- Ten original duals are the expensive part of the mana base; the alternative is the other five shocks and triomes.
- White and blue are the thinnest colours (15 land sources each) for Niv-Mizzet, Fact or Fiction and Austere Command, and Exotic Orchard depends on what the table's lands make.
- Whether the top end (Utvara Hellkite at eight, Summon: Bahamut at nine, Steel Hellkite, Niv-Mizzet) is too heavy now that Chrome Mox and Farseek are gone, and whether Young Pyromancer earns his slot with seventeen instants and sorceries.
- Protection is thin after the swap (Fierce Guardianship, Teferi's Protection, Dragonlord Ojutai): the deck's known weakness is a board wipe after it has committed its Dragons.
- Whether the three tutors (Demonic, Vampiric, Worldly) make the Garth combo too consistent for the table.
